# World and mystery rules

Draft v1. These rules establish what a later screenplay may show. New powers or mechanisms need a revision here before being used to solve a problem.

## Sunhollow

A communal meerkat settlement built into the shoulder of a sheltered desert ridge. Soft earthen homes and woven shades sit around older stonework. Daily life includes shared water, repair work, food preparation and expeditions between safe routes. Warmth and habitation should be visible before the danger grows.

This is a fantasy desert with mineral formations that store daylight, luminous seams deep inside the ridge and a shelter network that can move immense stone structures. Plants and creatures are invented but behave consistently. The principals learn through observation, materials, tools and cooperation.

The visible desert is beautiful and substantial: wind-carved ridges, apricot sand, layered stone, sparse tough plants, amber sunset and clear violet night. The underground world has cooler stone, water reflections and restrained amber light. Reserve the broadest magical vista for the first view into the old water gallery.

## Stable geography

The settlement and story are compact enough for a continuous afternoon adventure. No journey requires crossing a vast desert. Most locations are a few passages or a short surface walk apart. An evacuation lane remains usable throughout the climax.

The court's round basin, the gallery's wedge marks and the distribution chamber's central rib give viewers recognizable anchors. Rooms do not teleport. Rotating sleeves connect existing neighboring passages in different combinations. Do not cut through stone as if walls have become intangible.

The lookout is reached by a surface path; the network never creates it or moves it. It remains accessible in the final chapter. Some lower homes connect to the old relief bed; Upper Hearth and its ramp do not.

## The ancient shelter network

The original builders used stored light, water and wind pressure to route air and water around seasonal storms. The network rotates stone passage sleeves and changes channel gates. Its purpose is shelter, but it responds to physical conditions rather than understanding that later generations built homes in an old relief route.

Sunstone seams hold a limited charge gathered through surface light wells. The current cycle uses that charge. No character creates new energy, bonds with a crystal or obtains a hidden magical ability. The control chamber's stone relief shows channels, entrances and flow directions through shapes and moving light; there is no speaking control system or readable ancient text to translate on demand.

The original builders' identities do not need a separate subplot. Their practical design can be understood from repeated shapes, wear, flows and a small mechanical model.

## Why it activates today

Several months earlier, residents sealed an apparently unused western shutter to keep blowing grit away from occupied space. Mara knows about this repair. Its pale clay mortar and ochre binding cord are recognizable elsewhere in the settlement.

Since then, the western pressure outlet has remained closed. Today's first strong seasonal wind is the first to demand a full protective routing cycle. Pressure and stored light activate the system, but the intended outlet cannot open. The mechanism keeps progressing toward an emergency relief configuration through the lower channels.

The repair solved a real local problem. It was made without understanding the outlet's older function. The mistake belongs to the settlement's accumulated loss of knowledge; identifying it gives residents something useful they can help correct.

## Observable rules

1. **Warning before motion.** A local passage emits three low notes as light travels along its seams. Dust lifts from its joints. The door sleeve then moves to one of three physical alignments. Characters have time to get out of a threshold when they attend to the warning.
2. **Completion after the obvious sound.** After a moving section settles, a quieter return tick confirms that its latch is seated. The characters first overlook this, then learn it. Acting between the third note and the tick is unsafe.
3. **Movement has a footprint.** Wedge marks and worn floor arcs show where a sleeve will move. Fixed wall sockets and maintenance rims lie outside that arc. A marked fixed ledge can be trusted even when the center passage changes.
4. **Flow goes somewhere.** Holding one sleeve blocks a route and transfers pressure to another part of the network. A local jam cannot safely stop the whole system.
5. **The western outlet is necessary but insufficient.** Releasing it restores a safe pressure exit; it does not reverse occupied relief channels automatically. The distribution chamber must also select a compatible new configuration.
6. **A safe configuration has a cost.** The new configuration preserves the eastern court, workshop, Upper Hearth and water access. It reclaims the lowest inhabited lane as a relief channel. Residents and possessions must be moved first. The old arrangement cannot be restored under the new pressure conditions.
7. **Controls are linked.** At the distribution chamber, a small test lever models the routes before the full handle is committed. The final handle can only latch after the western shutter has opened and the return tick has sounded.
8. **Communication is physical.** Maintenance tubes carry voices or simple reed-note signals between the distribution chamber and the western outlet. Paired test ports in the turning gallery demonstrate sound passing between adjoining sections. One longer connection is clogged and repaired before it is needed. No telepathy, sudden radio or perfect shouting through solid stone.

Ordinary stone fracture, pressure, rope and stored energy create obstacles; the film need not present real engineering instructions. Keep the rules understandable through a few repeated actions.

## The clock

The storm wind strengthens through the afternoon. A visible marker in the distribution chamber moves toward its final relief position. The group can see that the next major pressure surge will send water through the occupied lower lane. This is the climax's clock, rather than an arbitrary countdown voice.

Small local movements happen at different times. Do not imply a fixed global cycle every few seconds: that would make travel and conversation implausible. The final release is one visible, continuous sequence across chapters 13–14. Exterior light remains late afternoon through chapter 15; chapter 16 is explicitly the next morning.

## The solution

Residents evacuate the lowest lane to Upper Hearth under Mara's direction. Tavi tests a new configuration that routes relief water through that emptied lane while retaining a dry connection to the court and a new upper passage.

Tavi and Nima work together at the distribution chamber. At the western outlet, Sera reaches the shutter release using fixed sockets; Kesh controls the shutter's brake with a counterweighted rope assembly. Tavi's clamp has already been removed from the gallery.

They use the maintenance tube to coordinate. Sera opens the western shutter in controlled steps while Kesh prevents a sudden swing. Opening it relieves pressure and allows the distribution interlock to complete its three-note sequence. Nima listens for that interlock's return tick rather than mistaking the loud third note for completion. At her signal, Tavi commits the tested configuration. Pressure discharges into the empty western wash; water takes the emptied lower relief lane. The dry ramp remains open. The climax's decisive tick is one clear event at the distribution chamber, after the outlet opens.

The action requires observations and choices established earlier. The film's victory is a safe new arrangement, achieved through four complementary kinds of cleverness and the community's work.

## Tone and consequences

Give peril space to feel real: a route closing behind them, water rising, an uncertain crossing, a decision made before perfect certainty. The group can be frightened and occasionally angry. Physical stakes remain legible, without graphic injury or deaths in this draft.

Comedy belongs to behavior and contrasts in perspective. A joke can release pressure once a character is safe; it should not erase another character's distress. The romance belongs to the same world of attentive gestures and imperfect communication.

The transformed home has visible losses and gains: displaced households, a submerged lower lane, a preserved communal heart, a working water route and a new upper entrance. Show residents beginning useful work and retaining familiar possessions. The ending's warmth comes from a believable path forward.
